Third-party sourcing

For buyers who know the specification but not the supplier. Nine steps from requirement to delivery follow-up, with nothing promised before it is checked.

The sourcing workflow

Each step exists because skipping it is where sourcing usually goes wrong.

  1. Receive buyer requirements

    Product, quantity, destination and the commercial terms you work to.

  2. Understand product specifications

    Ambiguity is resolved here rather than at inspection. Drawings, part numbers and samples all help.

  3. Identify potential suppliers

    Usually several, so the offer is a comparison rather than a single take-it-or-leave-it price.

  4. Review product and documentation availability

    What each supplier can actually issue: test reports, certificates of origin, phytosanitary documents, conformity declarations.

  5. Compare suitable sourcing options

    On specification match, documentation, lead time and landed cost together, not on unit price alone.

  6. Coordinate quotation

    A written offer restating the specification, so the thing quoted and the thing ordered are the same document.

  7. Confirm commercial terms

    Incoterm, payment terms, packing, inspection arrangements and what happens if the lot is rejected.

  8. Coordinate order and logistics

    Production or dispatch followed up, transport booked to the agreed terms.

  9. Follow up documentation and delivery

    Documents checked against the destination requirement before they travel, and the consignment tracked to arrival.

Supplier availability, pricing, product specifications, lead times and documentation are confirmed on a case-by-case basis. No delivery date, price or quality level is guaranteed outside a signed commercial agreement.

Good enquiries include

Part number or drawing, quantity, destination port or city, target timeline, and any standard the product is audited against.

What slows things down

Missing destination, unspecified grade, “best price” with no quantity, or a specification that changes after the supplier has quoted.

When we say no

Where the destination rules cannot be met, the quantity is uneconomic to ship, or no supplier can issue the documentation you need.
